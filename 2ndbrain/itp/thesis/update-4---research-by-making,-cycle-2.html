refresher on my image-generation models

ModelRoleNotes
SDXL-TurboPrimary candidateFast because it needs 1 to 4 steps, not because it’s small. Memory is close to full SDXL, so it’s the riskiest fit for the Pi’s 8 GB.
SD-TurboFallbackA smaller sibling of SDXL-Turbo.
SD 1.5FallbackThe best-supported and cheapest for LoRA training, and the likeliest to run acceptably on the Pi, at some cost in image quality. I called this a real argument against SDXL-Turbo.
OnnxStreamFallbackThis is a runtime, not a model. It runs Stable Diffusion models with less memory on the Pi. If you use it with a LoRA, merge the LoRA before converting.
i plan to run these on my mac through t3_image.py, a diffusers script, which uses SDXL-Turbo with PyTorch on the mac’s GPU (MPS)

onnxstream is another program like diffusers, built to use very little memory. backup opotion for Pi if PyTorch uses too much memory

results of first image benchmark test of just sd-turbo

(all 12 images are from sd-turbo)

how the test worked

  • warm-up: it made one throwaway images so the timings aren’t skewed by first-run setup (takes awhile to download the model from hugging face for the first time)
  • 12 images. each subject was put into the template “solid black silhouette of {subject}, side view, flat black shape on a plain white background, paper cutout, no texture, no shading”
  • results logged: the script recorded the time per image, the dark fraction (share of dark pixels), and whether an image came out all balck.
    • images in outputs/sd-turbo/2026-11-02 113622/

timing results

StepsTime per image
1~0.46 s
2~0.58 s
4~0.86 s
  • speed: fast and very consistent. each extra step adds about 0.13s.
    • these are times for my mac; the pi runs on its CPU and will be much slower
  • nothing failed: no all-black images and nothing flagged by the safety filter
  • dark fraction: between 0.11-0.47. it varies more with the seed than with the step count (boat with seed 1: 0.11, seed 2: 0.25). the seed decides how the scene is laid out; more steps mostly refine it.

what the images look like

  • the silhouettes themselves are good: clean, black solid shapes that are easy to read
  • the background isn’t plainm even though the prompt asks for it. the lantern image has a speckled paper texture, the 1-step boat has grey horizontal noise, and the 4-step boat added an island and stylised water.
  • prompt accuracy is loose: “a grandmother and child” came out as three people, and the lantern is white and see-through rather than part of the silhouette. it hardly even looks like a lantern
  • my negative prompt did nothing. sd-turbo runs with guidance at 0, so it ignores negative prompts (run_info.txt notes this). words like “no texture” in the main prompt also tend to work poorly.

takeaways

sd-turbo is fast and produces usable silhouettes. its weak spot is clean backgrounds. if my pipeline needs a pure white background, the easiest fix is a post-processing step that thresholds each image to pure black and white, rather than more prompt tweaking. next, i could run --model sdxl-turbo --limit 2 the same way to compare quality and speed.

results of sdxl-turbo

speed

  • speed is about 1.5-1.7x slower per image
  • memory: uses more than twice as much.
    • it may not fit on 8 GB Pi, but how about 16 GB? (borrow Cody’s)

image quality

sdxl-turbo is clearly better at silhouettes

  • clean backgrounds: without sd-turbo’s speckle, grain or added scenery
  • solid, readable shapes: with good detail (though i don’t think detail is so valuable)
  • better prompt-following
  • steadier across seeds: for the lantern prompt, the dark fraction was 0.31-0.33 for both seeds, versus 0.31 vs 0.47 for sd-turbo

still has some of the same problems:

  • the lantern isn’t black: it came out see-through in one image and glowing yellow in another, which breaks the “flat black shape” rule
  • extra people: the 4-step version has a grandmother and two children
  • the 1-step boat is very simplified: basically a black crescent. the 4-step boat looks much better but has grey water reflections

image quality

  • it’s the only model that used my negative prompt
  • grey, textured backgrounds that look like photos or paper, never plain white
  • shapes not solid black.
  • poor prompt-following

so far, technically

  • sdxl-turbo makes the best silhouettes, but its 9.9 GB of memory may be too much for the Pi
  • sd-turbo is probably the best fit for the Pi. it’s fast, needs ~4.6 GB, and its shapes are good. a step that converts each image to pure black and white afterwards would fix its messy backgrounds
  • sd15 is hard to justify. its about 7-14x slower, and its images are the least silhouette-like. on the Pi’s CPU, 20 steps per image will be very slow.

-> converting each image to pure black and white afterwards would still help both models, mainly with the colored lantern and the grey reflections

-> even on the 16 GB Pi, sdxl-turbo may be too big
sd-turbo and sd15 are ok though

with a 16 GB Pi, plan around sd-turbo. it runs without much swap use.

how this drives my thesis, conceptually

  • not satisfied with the image generation so far; quality isn’t there, i’m not proud to share it with my family as something that could represent them
  • my test prompts never mentioned anything in vietnamese
  • my metrics for assessing models is kinda generic
    • none of them ask whether a vietnamese family would recognize the image as theirs, or as characteristically vietnamese

things have already been lost with mistranslations, flattened translations

  • gemma3:1b turned rước đèn với bà (“carrying lanterns with grandma”) into “going to drink with my grandmother.”
  • gemma4:e2b, the model Google’s Gemma Translator uses, gave “I miss when little girls used to carry lanterns with Grandma.” The speaker disappeared from her own memory. That’s “how much can we be seen” in a single line of output.
  • Even the best version (gemma3 4B) says “carry lanterns.” It loses that rước đèn is a procession, and that the full moon points to Trung Thu, the Mid-Autumn Festival.

my installation itself is a lantern with a six-slot procession of images… the form could restore what that translation drops. how do i make that connection explicit?

how Family Intelligence makes my position clearer

Family Intelligence’s approach is preservation through structure. Their LLM turns unstructured stories into a maintainable, searchable archive, and the bet is that hardware keeps getting faster. We both agree that memories must be “embodied, as objects of heritage” and not only live in a phone.

Where we differ:

AspectFamily IntelligenceMy thesis
GoalCapture everything and make it searchableShow what gets through and what doesn’t
HardwareBet that it improvesUse the weakest hardware on purpose

TimePermanent archiveSix slots, permanent archive

the grap is what makes my thesis worth pursuing, not a weakness. their tool asks people to trust the archive, whereas mine lets people see the compression.

  • vietnamese speech-to-text
  • translation from vietnamese text to english text
  • then a short subject

    -> each step is a translation where something is lost.

considerations of where it could fall flat

  • “AI flattens culture” is a common critique. it only works if i show the specific losses i actually observed, like the lantern or the vanished “I,” rather than claim it in general
  • the pipeline hides its own losses. if peoples only see a silhouette, they can’t tell what was lost. consider showing the original vietnamese next to it, or letting people hear the gap between what was said and what appeared
  • deliberate abstraction and that model’s defaults are different things. a silhouette leaves room for someone’s own memory, and that’s good
    • cite “in defense of the poor image” by hito steyerl
    • cite “in defense of the silhouette” by yoona
    • cite “understanding comics”
    • look for more texts on compression
