update 6 - thesis proposal outline

Nov 18, 2026

things i want to emphasize

  • drawing inspiration from
    • family.intelligence
      • tenets:
        • timeless (lasts across generations)
        • observable (easy to control)
        • trustworthy (trusted from the first use on)
    • not the only one by stephanie dinkins
    • i knew that if i walked in your footsteps, it would become a ritual by aarati akkapeddi
    • google gemma translator
    • heirloom hardware by modem
  • i also want my project to be speculative research on local llms for art objects for families toe benefit from
  • ai should be local, specialized, and idiosyncratic — models have never been this small and hardware this accessible which is why i feel compelled to explore this terrain during the extended period of thesis
  • the idea of “loss of roots” when it comes to diaspora— outside of vietnam and even within vietnam when someone moves from the countryside to the big city
    • my personal thesis is that it’s beneficial to connect with your roots — your culture/heritage, family history — to live a more connected and less independent life
    • the history tells the future
  • i’m building upon others’ efforts to document and archive stories of the vietnamese diaspora — vietnamese boat people, the vietnamese podcast by kenneth nguyen
  • i want to, in a non-condescending way, talk about how my family members with lack of formal education and language barriers have challenges with media literacy
    • i’ve personally seen them fall victim to AI and online scams; its deepened my interest in safety, trust, disinformation, interaction design and how it fits into and shapes our culture
    • on one hand i want to make a suspension of disbelief object— i am a big fan of folklore and of magical realism. its a big part of vietnamese culture. i was recently reading “vietnamese legends and folktales” and gabriel garcia marquez’s “chronicle of a death foretold” sticks with me a lot
    • but i also want to bring attention to how these technologies of our time are not as objective, perfect, ideal as they are portrayed to be, to a non-technical audience
      • things like taste, hallucination
        • but i ask myself are these worth surfacing to the user?
  • what exactly am i trying to say in using AI to try to help disparate generations of family understand and connect each other through understanding their shared history?
    • “the root”

stephanie dinkins’ Not the Only One main three points:

  1. communities should build and own their own AI
    • care
    • consent
    • co-creation need to be built into these AI systems
  2. small, specific data matters more than polish — similar to aarati’s handmade datasets work
  3. the archive talks back
    • rather than a library you search
    • a new kind of conversant archive that you speak with
      • a small LoRA trained on consented family material would be my version of dinkins’ move

compelling as a project to explore new ways of connecting with our roots?

A LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) is a small add-on file that nudges a big model toward a particular style or subject without retraining the whole thing.
