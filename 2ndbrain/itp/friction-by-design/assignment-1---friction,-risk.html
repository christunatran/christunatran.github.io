assignment 1 - friction, risk

Oct 28, 2026

Make an interactive digital project that uses AI to take an input, show computation, and output something. It should be accompanied by a short essay (min 300 words) describing the technology used, the strategic method applied, the user experience at the core, the risk, and the friction being applied (to be presented in class). 

  • Use friction in any method discussed in class to prevent a risk to the user - this risk may be real, presumed, hypothetical or contextual.
  • Be ready for a design review and to present a formed rationale
  • Please detail your choice of platform in your presentation

brainstorming

risks to user:

  • information overload; not actually learning anything, skimming through text/media
    • proneness to misinformation, propaganda
  • learning in isolation; no collaboration
  • bad decisions from the need for speed
  • compulsive context-switching — “unexamined context-switching, leading to fragmented attention”
    • impulse without intention
    • fragmented attention
    • unexamined switching

thoughts

  • there’s a difference between good friction vs. bad friction
    • good friction: added on purpose to force reflection, prevent errors, build security, increase perceived value (?)
    • bad friction: poor execution or unneeded complexity that hurts usability, causes cognitive overload, and drives users away
  • make the computation visible
  • should use AI in some way to 1) take an input 2) show computation 3) produce an output

ideas

problem

how to fight the compulsion that is too easily satisfied from seamless/frictionless UX design -> impulsive context-switching without reflection

  • this gap between impulse and action has collapsed into almost nothing
  • Affordance and the gulf of execution (don norman): the gesture affords switching so well that the gulf between wanting and doing disappears.
    • friction reintroduces a small, deliberate gulf
    • we once had to get up and actively move to another part of the room to perform a task, with intention and a physical ‘gulf’
  • bush’s proposal of the memex is not just about being fast
    • the core feature is the associative trail— deliberately linked items, naming the trail, keeping it, and sharing it
    • every jump was a deliberate act of choosing a connection and left a record
    • modern tab and desktop switching keeps the speed and drops the trail
      -> we inherited bush’s speed of retrieval without his structure of intention
  • in-line with virilio, “every invention invents its accident”
    • compulsive switching is not a malfunction of the mac interface
    • it’s the accident that frictionless switching invented, and the product produces it by working exactly as designed
    • the problem is structural, not a matter of users lacking self-control
      • it is bad design to put the onus on the user!
  • position: not exactly that “frictionless design is bad”
    • speed needs orientation, don’t reject fast interfaces

proposal

  • ‘intend’ app that forces you to give a reason before checking your email or other website
  • an app that turns your computer into a ‘dumb computer’
    • for a set span of time for you to complete a task
  • if your AI asks for a reason/intention before each switch, those reasons can become a trail
    • over a session, the model could build a memex-like record of where your attention went and why, and show it back to you
  • have to physically get up before switching a task, in a feynman-technique kind of way…
    1. be in the middle of a task
    2. get a compulsion to switch to another task
    3. get blocked from switching to the next task
    4. have to get up — camera tracking
    5. have to speak out loud what you want to switch to
    6. get prompted to reflect if it is urgent; if not, then save it for later

essay-writing, sources

  • Interaction cost (Nielsen Norman Group): the physical and mental effort an action requires.
    • my claim: the interaction cost of context-switching has been driven toward zero.
  • The “machine zone” (Natasha Dow Schull, Addiction by Design): the trance-like state where continuous, uninterrupted interaction between the goal
    • in reference ot casinos and slot machine design
    • swiping between desktops produces a mild version of this— motion without destination
  • Attention residue (sophie leroy): part of your attention stays stuck on the previous task after you switch, so frequent switching degrades performance even when each switch feels free
  • Continuous partial attention (Linda stone): a chronic state of scanning everything while committing to nothing
    • risk: skimming, not learning
  • Affordance and the gulf of execution (don norman): the gesture affords switching so well that the gulf between wanting and doing disappears.
    • friction reintroduces a small, deliberate gulf
