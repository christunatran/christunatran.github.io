11-09-2025

I hate white america

(disclaimer: i am a yuppie transplant with a significant amount of privilege that allows me to live, work, and study in a place like new york city.)

i’m convinced that those who subscribe to the neoliberal, capitalistic and individualistic culture that exists in america don’t and may never experience what community could be. how can you? when the way you grow up is exactly as the “american dream” pushes: nuclear family with 1-2 kids, behind a white picket fence in a car-dependent suburb, “friends” and family we hardly see because they’re too busy tending to their individual lives to even be depended on to drive you to the airport. a lot of kids i’ve met from affluent backgrounds who didn’t get to grow up in big extended families or ethnic enclaves don’t know how to properly share. especially in white america, it leads to a personality shaped around consumerism to compensate for a lack of culture, leading to a over-emphasis of ownership of material objects… “what’s mine and what’s yours”. it’s sad that in Vietnam, they often view Vietnamese-Americans as people who have “lost their roots”. i don’t disagree, in a lot of ways i feel white-washed too and now i wish i could somehow undo it.

i don’t mean to romanticize living in community either. there are points where with my family, and in Vietnam, that i struggle to have privacy or independent thought, because it constantly feels like people are projecting their opinions and voices in my ears.

in a way, keeping others at arm’s reach feels like avoidance. it’s safer to people-please and remain at the surface level. there’s not enough time nor energy in a day to ruffle any feathers. conflict is scary!!! here, we’d rather be lonely than learn how to compromise.

i remember when i was in middle school and trying to do my homework in my bedroom while my parents were loudly singing karaoke in the other room. i complained to Uncle Thai and he admonished me, sharing his experience of having immigrated to america at my age as a part of a family of 7, squished into a government-subsidized studio apartment in washington, dc, with only enough room to lay out several mattresses on the floor, where everyone slept and spent time together. there was no privacy, but they survived. this moment taught me that there are times that you have to compromise for community.

i don’t mean to glorify their struggle either. i think their circumstances inhibited them in some ways. for instance, a lot of my family members shit on the same Uncle Thai for doing poorly in school. but how could he have done well without having a quiet place, or a proper desk to study at home? but the same circumstances that they struggled through, together as a family allowed them to remain close to this day.

new york city and other forms of privilege

new york city causes you to objectify other people. whether in dating where people and interactions are gamified into swipes on a screen, or people you call friends coming and going, whether its from being “too busy” or literally moving away. it often feels like you have to be an asshole because kindness somehow gets interpreted as a sign that you’re a pushover. in the train stations, we are conditioned to ignore homeless people. it’s not an unusual sight to witness people overdosing on the street. it’s sad to have become so desensitized to the normalization of… human beings’ suffering.

(disclaimer again: i am a yuppie transplant with a significant amount of privilege that allows me to live, work, and study in a place like new york city.)

i find a lot of my kind (lol) to be largely performative and i’m still trying to process how i feel about it. i find it really difficult to take people seriously when they move to a place for not even a year, live in an ivory tower high-rise doorman building, hang out exclusively with people from their private school alma mater and tax-bracket, and post profusely on their instagram story about nyc politics. to simultaneously exist in a bubble and to pretend to be mad about class and inequality makes no sense to me. i find that the biggest yappers and virtue-signalers are not the ones out there canvassing for or donating to [insert the currently trending political candidate here]‘s campaign. no, they’re way too busy!!! …going to marketing pop-up events to get free shit that ends up taking up even more space in an overflowing apartment with all the other hoarded junk and buying $9 matcha (it’s me! i’m a hypocrite. i’m disgusted at myself). it really is disgusting the way that Americans treat their homes as storage units rather than a home with intention and utility and space to walk around in the storage room. it’s exhausting to live with these karens and NIMBYs-in-training where you’re made to feel like you have to negotiate for every inch of space just because they had been there first. (history really repeats itself because that’s the same rhetoric the yts use, totally ignoring what they did to the native americans) and they don’t even see the power imbalances in these situations.

obligatory white ppl rant

i think that white americans like my roommate are so unaware of just how much space they take up figuratively and literally. from dominating conversations and being condescending to BIPOC who don’t conform to their idea of whiteness being the baseline for the default or “the norm” (can you imagine if all BIPOC conformed to white as the norm? that would be the most tasteless, bland, boring, and flat reality to live in) or what’s acceptable or literally the sheer amount of consumerism and materialistic bullshit that their culture encourages them to engage in. they like to act like the police officers of the world, which america has a history of doing (ahem, theodore roosevelt, big stick diplomacy, panama, ahem). white americans don’t understand just how laughable their ‘history’ and their ‘culture’ is. it’s clearly not a model for other countries, just look at the obesity and education stats in this place; it’s rough out here. the one that’s the most embarrassing but the one that lacks humility the most, going further with being self-aggrandizing, self-victimizing, subscribing to the snowflake-participation-trophy-culture that expects constant external validation from others for accomplishing little to nothing. i know that the way to get on people’s good side here i have to praise them when they say shit like “you know what, i’m so proud of how far i’ve come” like a line from a shitty coming-of-age movie, and it’s my own fault for rejecting it and coming across as rude in their eyes. it’s hard to respect someone when this country socialized them into an agent of white supremacy.

they try to defend themselves because maybe they have some BIPOC friends, but again, those friends are often white-washed to be able to put up with the whiteness.

i find that the only enjoyable white people to be around are the ones who have significantly distanced themselves from the american corporate neoliberal individualistic materialistic machine and have freed themselves from their whiteness through art or spirituality or whatever. ones who are not spiritually chopped.

if america never intervened in the vietnam war (which the vietnamese actually refer to as “the american war”) then my family would’ve never had to uproot our lineage and end up here lol. i often hate it here and have to put in great effort to find things to like. i never asked to be here, but pragmatically it doesn’t make sense to leave at this stage in my life. my family and roots are here. love and hate—two truths can exist at the same time, but like with a lot of things, this country struggles with nuance.

an ex-boyfriend once commented that i’m “racist toward white people”. lol. but i argue that white americans are racist every day, most of the time not even intentionally, but due to sheer ignorance. even when they claim to enjoy aspects of other cultures— their attempts end up half-baked and more often than it’s appropriation at best. i’m looking at the asiaboos. i’m also looking at my fallen asian brothers and sisters with a high proximity to whiteness (white-washing lol, WAKE UP). i pour one out for the ones who end up as the token asian/non-white in an all-white friend group because i know they’re going through some form of self-loathing that makes them wish they were white, too. surely, with the token white in an asian friend group (the asiaboo), the white one doesn’t wish they were asian.

like julia lee in “biting the hand: growing up asian in black and white america”, i am angry, a lot. what society collectively needs to heal is for white people to stop acting up.

what this book has to say about “reverse-racism”:

  • Racial minorities have not historically held systemic power in Western societies like the United States.
  • Individual acts of prejudice by a minority person against a white person do not equate to the centuries of historical, systemic oppression and institutional marginalization that people of color have faced.
  • The term “reverse racism” is often used to distract from or deflect conversations about systemic racism and racial equity initiatives.

racism is a function of prejudice + institutional power

in practicing “humanizing the enemy” and empathy, what often goes through my head when i have a conflict is “aw, i guess they can’t help that they’re so white.” …for better or for worse. but it’s what keeps me sane and allows me to move on from the stupid ass shit they say and do.

something insidious is when rather than acknowledging their own privilege they grasp at straws to try to find it in others. unprompted, so quick to try to lower others with “it’s such a privilege you get to attend grad school” maybe you could attend too, if you planned ahead, saved your money and you stopped buying Tik Tok Shop-Temu-Teso Life junk? this shit is not free. it’s suuuch a privilege to dig into my 3+ years of savings to pay this exorbitant tuition to this private, morally ambiguous institution. shit, am i upholding white supremacy by attending here?

other thoughts on privilege…

university is a place for laundering privilege… meritocracy is fake… (parents’) fortune favors the brave. these are thoughts that have made me feel really frustrated but i guess i’ve improved in the sense that i don’t allow them to ruin my day as much as they once did.

we’re all gentrifiers here, this is a city built upon invading and displacing people. it’s definitely not good, but it’s a difficult truth that we shouldn’t try to avoid. even the native americans technically immigrated here on the land bridge. there’s no need to push that the owner at the nearby bodega has finally learned your name to validate that you’re a “real new yorker”, that you’re “not a gentrifier”, that you’re somehow better than those white gentrifiers living down the hall from you in your overpriced building. we’re all cosplaying in this city that’s cosplaying as what it used to be.

america, and probably the world, is doomed if we keep down this path of 100% remote bullshit jobs. we can’t be attempting to find meaning in our material objects and premium mediocre consumption. but sometimes shelling out your wages for the sake of signaling taste and striving in the food you eat without requiring deep knowledge or being genuinely luxury items is fun and makes you feel like you’re somehow better than those who don’t care <3.

this became a rant. i’m probably a people-hater with a superiority complex.
