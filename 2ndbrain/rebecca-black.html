Rebecca Black

Aug 29, 2026

thoughts critical haterpee
yesterday, rebecca black’s “friday” resurfaced on my social media feed. it was worse than i remembered. when it came out in 2011, i remember watching it with my cousins and talking about it with uncle thai.

uncle thai said:

“this girl knows how to sing, but she has no talent for it.”

rebecca came from a well-off family, with both parents being veterinarians. they paid ARK Music Factory $4000 for the music video. presumably, she also had singing lessons to learn the techniques for singing, but her innate voice ends up not being pleasant to listen to. now, she is a DJ who has performed at Boiler Room.

this reminded me of my ex boyfriend whose wealthy parents, even wealthier than black’s, bankrolled each and every one of his short films. i liked him a lot but the writing of his films reeked of being soulless, out-of-touch, and spiritually chopped… makes sense when you had such a sterile and privileged upbringing— no character.

people like that get to LARP as an artist or director without having to build their way up working as a PA or intern, or save up their own money. it’s easier to fail upward when you have a portfolio crediting yourself as someone who produced all of these works involving big crews and resources— most people don’t have that opportunity— inevitably, you stand out. it just goes to show how unmeritocratic these industries are.

realization:

shit, i was dating the male chinese equivalent of rebecca black.

as i mentioned in discussions on rich people and art, me being salty enough about this to write about it definitely is a form of passivity in disguise. but at the same time it motivates me to work harder. weird.

there is a funny comment my friend MT makes when a rich person pisses them off:

“they’re dying first in the revolution!”
