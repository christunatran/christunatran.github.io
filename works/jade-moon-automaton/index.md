## artist statement

Poet Hàn Mặc Tử wrote to the moon as if it were a person one could follow and yearn for. Written during the leprosy isolating him at the end of his short life, his poems “Đây thôn Vĩ Dạ” ("Here is Vi Da Hamlet") and “Trăng vàng trăng ngọc” ("Golden Moon, Jade Moon") ache with longing for something that visits but won’t stay. Nearly a century later, singer Phương Mỹ Chi's “Buông Trăng” ("Letting Go of the Moon") was inspired by these poems and carried these lines to this generation.

This automaton is my attempt to carry them into the physical form. Turning the hand crank makes the moon rise and set over painted mountains, while a small boat drifts beneath it, following it left to right through the mist.

Every time, the moon sets, and the viewer is the one carrying it.

One way of inheriting traditions is by remaking them. A poem became a song, a song became an automaton, all continuing to keep the moon just out of reach.
