it's a harsh way of thinking, but i feel like i used to be such a loser. spending so much time in my head, not leaning into action, being negative and focusing on others rather than myself. when i behave in this way, i consider it my **loser behavior**. it fills me with insecurity. and i think that what they say about "energy attracts" is true. i find that when i lean into optimism not as being delusional, but rather intentionally being ruthlessly optimistic as a resistance to how much the world sucks, i attract so many wonderful people and events into my life. it makes me more confident to get to know the people i respect and admire, and to no longer, out of loneliness, accept 'friends' into my life even when they make me feel bad. ABUNDANCE MINDSET hahaha.

i find a lot of people in my program hard to respect. in a conversation with a friend, the explanation they gave was, is "it's art school, most of the people here have to be rich... to **be** in art school in the first place". a lot of my peers grew up going to fancy boarding schools, international schools, and fine art programs before ending up at NYU. a lot have never worked actual jobs before, and/or are still getting financially supported by their parents. i often feel envious about my peers who have gotten to pursue art ever since they were young. to have never been told "no" or to pursue STEM or something that actually makes money. this envy is a careful line i walk because it easily becomes resentment. resentment or observation? there is a classmate that i really struggle to respect. their parents support their entire tuition, rent, and other living expenses. so they don't have to work, while i'm currently working 3 student jobs and living off of my own savings. it's really jarring when they're constantly complaining about "oh i don't know what to do", "i don't know where to start", etc. hearing them complain about this and that just rubs me as first-world problems. my mistake was collaborating with this person. i find that they don't have any grit-- they give up too easily when they encounter the inevitable obstacle. their life is so inconsequential that they easily just give up and go home, even when it affects their partner. what's even more jarring is the frequency that they skip class, which i guess is easy to do when you don't have to think about how much each of those classes cost. despite not having any job or extracurriculars to worry about outside of class, this person would oversleep for a meeting at *11:30AM*. it was really disappointing to see. but maybe most jarring was when this person told me that they "sometimes just contemplate giving up and opening a cafe back at home." with what money? oh right, rich parents. 

i think that i do hold some amount of resentment toward rich international students. i find the plight of an international student trying to stay in the US unreal and hard to empathize with, even though a lot of my friends are wonderful people. ultimately, to me, studying abroad, and having the *choice* over where you live and work as a matter of preference (i.e. preferring US work culture over China's) is an immense privilege. i think about how my family was forced to immigrate here to escape persecution from my grandpa being on the losing side of the war and spending 5 whole years of his life as a prisoner of war... and we've somehow ended up here due to the American guilt of fucking up our motherland, Vietnam.

there is also something to be said about how rich people can afford to pathologize their problems almost as a way to avoid solving them. i had a wealthy coworker who got extensive (and expensive) sleep studies done to diagnose him with a light case of narcolepsy. basically, for him, whenever he got stressed he would feel tired and sleepy. my harsh, unempathetic thought was, "doesn't everyone get tired and sleepy?". but the root of this judgement is that not everyone can just afford to get a sleep study done. same with leaning into an OCD diagnosis to explain why you choose to live your life a certain way. i can only hope the diagnosis comes with some sort of intention to try to change yourself rather than an excuse to continue an unhealthy way of living?

while there are role models, i think i have more role models of people i *don't* want to be like, and that somehow manages to direct my life in a good way. 

back to focusing on myself and positive vibes.
