i spent over 13 hours at school today. arriving at 9:30 for my Shop shift, and staying until 11. yet, it was the most fulfilling day i've spent at NYU ITP thus far.

it began with the most relaxed Shop shift ever, unsurprisingly, because finals just ended and all that's left is for thesis and capstone presentations happening on the other side of the floor. scanning the room, the few people remaining are those who are finishing up their presentation decks or the extremely committed few who practically live on the floor regardless of what day it is. 

i was there to work on my breathing lamp and my group piece for the transit museum exhibition. joined by my collaborators matt and ryan, the day was spent locked in with breaks to watch interesting thesis presentations. unfortunately, i didn't go outside even once... the weather was muggy. 

i joked to matt that he would "see all of the phases of tunapee today". to which ray overheard and while laughing, said that she "really wants to see it." and they sure did... i typically avoid spending too much time with people, partially because my energy and mood fluctuates DRASTICALLY throughout the day. in the early morning, they witnessed my exhausted "i need coffee" state, then my post-coffee state where i was bouncing around and yelling whatever unhinged stupid ass shit came to mind, back to depressed-questioning-the-meaning-of-life-and-contemplating-giving-it-all-up, to pacing around and doing jumping jacks to wake up, to peak locked-in focus time. i'm grateful to these folks for witnessing me and being patient with me, especially matt and ryan for listening intently as i was incomprehensibly word-vomiting ideas for our project. when i get excited grammar just goes out the window.

i think that without these folks going through it with me, i wouldn't have had the motivation to keep going... it just goes to show how important it is to surround yourself with the right environment with similarly-motivated peers. looking around and seeing everyone else working fills me with energy.

i felt myself go through a second, third, maybe fourth wind on a final push to finish my lamp. it's exciting to get people's feedback as i'm working on it. it made me more excited to share the finished product right away (thanks fatma for helping me document, and thanks queenie for letting me pull you out of your work to take a look). 

i'm really glad that i kept pushing on this lamp. i learned so much about fabrication from all of the mistakes i made in CAD, woodworking, prototyping, 3D printing, inflatables, material exploration, lighting, mounting, it's all a blur. but this project was so adequately scoped for the skills i have + the skills i wanted to build. it's given me so much more confidence as a fabricator. i went to bed and woke up thinking about it. it fills me with energy, and it keeps the poisonous thoughts away. 

yesterday, i spent the whole day thinking about and working on a web art piece. when i look back on the last few days and weeks, i feel nothing short of fulfilled, even though most of it was spent working. i've been filled with so much adrenaline that i can't even eat properly, it's weird. i don't think that i could be producing at the rate that i've been had i still been in the relationship that i was in. i have so much brain space now that i'm not constantly worried about a man's uncertainty... aaahhh. days like these remind me that i live a fulfilling life filled with wonderful people and things when i'm on my own, so the only space that there is for a partner is one who can add to my life, not subtract. i have so much to build and to discover about myself as tunapee, the individual. so there's only room for someone really special.

the book "Why Greatness Cannot Be Planned: The Myth of the Objective" changed my life by challenging me to embrace being a "do-er". to experiment and follow my curiosity and serendipity rather than stick to rigid goal-oriented planning. i feel like lately, with the experiments and explorations i'm doing, in line with the motto i keep repeating to myself of "LESS THINK, MORE DO", i'm finally putting it into practice, and it's serving me so so so much. i hate how i used to spend so much time in my head, thinking that i had to perfectly plan everything out before starting. probably because i was afraid of making a mistake, of the sunk cost of time. but now, i'm seeing that the only way out is through. that there's always going to be lacking information, so i just need to start, experiment, fail FAST, learn from the failure, and iterate my way to the finish. 

i feel like i've found what i'm meant to do: making through exploring and experimenting, agnostic of medium, at the edge of my abilities.

and i'm getting better about documenting and sharing it all!
